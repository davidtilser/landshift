## Architecture and implemented behavior

- `education.ts` reads the size-aware teaching baseline and current chapter/proposal. The
  Water, Electricity and Land buttons open one idea at a time, with progress,
  Back/Next controls and explicit evidence labels. Unknown measurements remain
  unknown; the source reader retains the full explanation and source links.
- `ui-text.ts` splits long text at sentence/word boundaries without dropping
  content. Its limit is 70 Unicode code points per text page, including spaces.
  Public/private histories and model context retain the complete message.
  Reading pages pauses automatic community exchanges.
- `PoloScene.setEnvironmentalLayer()` selects water, electricity, land, heat or
  no explanatory overlay. Water and heat pipes use depth-fail materials to show
  a cutaway below the terrain. Native depth-independent pipe markers keep the
  cutaway visible through streamed meshes as well. Flow markers stop moving
  under reduced motion. Competing map labels are hidden while a layer is active;
  the people and portrait chat shortcuts remain available.
  Supply pipes, drainage and groundwater are never treated as equivalent.
- Existing heat-reuse geometry connects an exchanger to one **fictional pool**,
  with separate supply/return circuits, backup cooling and recipient availability.
  The new heat cutaway highlights that same schematic connection. It does not
  assert a real building connection or a measured reduction in heating demand.
- `impact.ts` compares the original plan and current view at the **same chapter,
  placement and baseline operating case**. Public turf lost also identifies Today
  as its reference. Resource savings for visual proposals remain unquantified.
- `experience.ts` provides a five-step impact reader. It appears after the
  operating transition and the first application of each proposal; it can also be
  opened manually. Reset clears the feedback history and overlay.
- Mobile sources and reset controls are available in the More menu.
  The main camera strip sits inside the top navbar and scrolls horizontally;
  dialogs retain native keyboard
  focus handling. Small screens use short map chat labels alongside portraits.
- Opposition starts when the campus proposal is visible, before construction.
  Existing peaceful rally animations remain anchored to the field. Stage and
  proposal reactions affect the rally without removing opposition to field loss.

## Simulation boundaries

The new Support value is an **authored 0–100 game index**, not an approval
percentage, real poll, calibrated population model or community consent. The
stage values are Today 16, Foundations 12, Structure 8, Ready 10, Operating 12.
At Operating, a visible heat concept adds 6 points only with an available
recipient; recycled water adds 4; a green roof adds 3. Selecting Original plan
removes those additions. No improvement makes building in the park acceptable
by default. Changes are displayed in **index points**, never percentage points.

At Operating, rally turnout falls by half the point gain, rounded down, from an
authored base of 20. At least 17 rally figures remain. The displayed **Crowd
emotions** percentages are a separate fictional emotional mix, updated through
the existing community conversation system. They are explicitly labeled as
separate from Support; neither metric measures actual residents.

The numerical baseline is still generated by the existing reproducible research
scripts. No new external environmental facts or local utility records were added.
The reference 4 ha / 12-million-L/year irrigation allowance is a teaching assumption; actual
park water supply is unknown. Proposal water, electricity and heat savings are
not inferred from the appearance of a pipe or planted roof.

## Backlog, in priority order

1. **Quantified proposal models:** validated input parameters for heat delivery,
   heat-pump electricity, recycled-water allocation/treatment and roof retention.
   Acceptance: reproduce baseline/alternative outputs with unit and boundary
   checks, show losses and benefits, and cite each parameter’s provenance.
2. **Verified local infrastructure:** utility routes, allocations, capacity and
   real consenting recipients. Acceptance: replace schematic routes only after
   documented access, licensing and engineering validation.
3. **Multiple heat recipients:** extend the existing single fictional pool to a
   configurable network. Acceptance: recipient availability and heat demand
   govern each delivery branch; no automatic neighborhood-wide savings.
4. **Combined decisions:** proposals currently remain independent previews.
   Acceptance: compose effects without double-counting water, energy or land.
5. **Community model validation:** expose/validate authored parameters with young
   participants and planning facilitators. Do not rename the index to approval
   or claim learning/behavior change without evidence.
6. **Older sandbox parity:** migrate Heroes’ Grove/San José information panels and
   remaining older gameplay copy to the same curriculum/readers. The new shared
   chat pagination already applies there; the entire older UI is not certified
   against the 70-character rule in this iteration.
7. **Broader onboarding:** the `/` entry remains the existing starter. The Polo
   game now includes the city-to-site introduction described below.
8. **Additional locations:** Mission Bay, Presidio and Treasure Island appear as
   coming-soon choices. Each needs its own geography, evidence and impact model
   before becoming playable; Golden Gate remains the only active selection.
9. **Optional size-model validation, deferred:** the demo uses the default 4 ha
   case. Existing alternate-size behavior remains unchanged; engineering work
   for other sizes is outside current demo acceptance. Future UI-only requests
   must not imply changes to underlying simulation complexity without checking
   that scope with the user.

## US audience display units

Polo's visible interface now uses acres for site/land areas, square feet for
building areas, US liquid gallons for water and fuel, inches for rain, and
feet/miles for distances. Electricity retains kWh, MWh, GWh and MW. The default
four-hectare campus displays as **10 acres**, with rounded **5–15 acres** slider
endpoints; the operating cooling allowance displays as **80k US gal/day**.
This is a presentation change: slider inputs,
the default 4 ha footprint, geometry and simulation calculations are unchanged.

`us-units.ts` owns display conversion and rounding. Whole numbers are preferred;
meaningful fractions, small nonzero quantities, rainfall depth and dimensionless
ratios retain useful precision. Large volumes and building areas use k/M suffixes
with their units and time bases intact. `display.ts` formats phase
cards without modifying their SI data or the frozen research JSON. Explanations
are converted before their 70-character pagination, while linked source titles
and URLs remain intact. Education, impact, map labels and chat facts use the same
formatters; the live-chat prompt requests the same public-facing units.

Land conventions were checked against [SF Planning](https://sfplanning.org/resource/planning-director-bulletin-no-8-streamlined-housing-development).
Conversions use the modern international foot/acre definitions from
[NIST](https://www.nist.gov/pml/us-surveyfoot/revised-unit-conversion-factors)
and the US liquid gallon, not the Imperial gallon. The retained electrical units
follow [EIA's measurement explanation](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/electricity/measuring-electricity.php).
Five focused tests verify conversion, rounding, retained SI data and US presentation across
all default chapters, lessons and comparison cards. All 192 tests and the build
pass; no new engineering model or alternate-size validation was introduced.

## Selected size, building framing and coherent conversations

`size.ts` keeps the frozen 4 ha reference intact. Other sizes scale horizontal
geometry by the square root of the area ratio, and teaching quantities by the
area ratio. Rain depth, workdays, unknown values and physical building heights
stay unchanged. The selected hectares persist through confirmation and every
chapter. Visible roofs, foundations, hard surfaces, land boundaries, access gate
and infrastructure routes use the same geometric transform. Non-default values
are labeled as area-proportional teaching assumptions; retained source prose is
explicitly labeled as the frozen 4 ha reference. This is not site engineering.

The default site camera uses `760 / 1.3` metres instead of 760 metres, scaled to
the selected site size. This makes the building about 30% more prominent without
inventing additional hectares. Desktop framing shifts the building away from
the story card. Whole-park and connection views keep space for their context.

Polo private chat now uses the data-driven branches in `polo/dialogue.ts`.
Sixteen question topics offer person-, chapter- and proposal-aware answers.
Every opening answer fits a complete short reading page.
Benefits questions name the potential computing users, displaced public use
and, when active, the fictional heat recipient. Related unasked questions replace
the previous suggestions after each turn. Custom text retains suggestions;
short questions such as **Why?** deepen the preceding topic. Unknown custom
topics receive an explicit limit rather than an unrelated canned answer.

Guided turns use an **Authored scenario** response. Configured custom replies
retain the live provider path; offline custom replies use the same coherent
local branches. Full messages and histories remain available with 70-character
reading pages. Keyed bubbles preserve each earlier message. Mobile compacts the
suggestion cards to leave more room for the conversation. Resize anchoring keeps
the latest reply visible while respecting a reader who has scrolled upward.

Size/model, shared contract, scene/route and dialogue regression tests cover the
new behavior. Browser evidence is in `docs/screenshots/size-chat/`.

## Community opinion bubbles

The map shows at most two conversation invitations. Maya voices the current
field-loss concern in coral; a mint card offers a constructive idea from Owen,
or Aisha when the green-roof concept is active. The idea is a question or
conditional possibility, not an invented approval vote. A missing heat recipient
changes the invitation accordingly. These teasers do not alter sentiment scores.

Each bubble includes a portrait, name, short opinion and **Let’s talk** action
opening that character’s existing private conversation. Other people remain
available in Community and the desktop portrait shortcuts. Mobile replaces the
duplicate portrait strip with two cards above the story deck. Desktop bubbles
follow their people and avoid the impact controls and story deck. When enough
desktop space exists, they share an upper row to keep the building visible. Map layers and
the public Community view hide competing invitations; the protest crowd remains.

Implementation: `src/game/polo/opinions.ts` contains authored invitations;
`opinion-bubbles.ts` and its stylesheet render accessible native buttons;
`scene.ts` supplies the current scenario and projected person positions. Evidence
is in `docs/screenshots/community-opinions/`.
