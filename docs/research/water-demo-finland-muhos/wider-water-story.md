# Muhos: the wider water story, kept simple

Use **one current connection per milestone**, alongside the existing campus scene and water card. Show evidence labels, and let users open detail without blocking the timeline. The key message is that changing ground and drainage can affect water before servers ever run.

## 1. Water moves even while construction is paused

At M1/M2, move from trees to disturbed ground and schematic ditches. The documented receiving river is **Oulujoki**. Highlight a generic control/monitoring point between the site and river; its exact location and proven effectiveness are not supplied.

A short default explanation is: “Rain and drainage connect the disturbed site to the river; water protection remains necessary during the pause.” The October LVV document provides the connection and current concerns. [LVV, printed pp.3–6](https://www.sttinfo.fi/files/69821631/72435808/532934/fi)

The optional rain replay uses the offsite FMI 40 mm daily record. Label any geometric rain volume **Gross rain, not runoff**. Snowmelt appears as an explanatory icon in details, not a summer-rain replay relabelled as a winter model.

## 2. Water quality has a specific local mechanism

Use a small inset: **sulfide-bearing material exposed during drainage/excavation → oxidation → potential acidic, metal-bearing runoff**. The regulator reports indications of acidification in company monitoring, but this packet has no numerical pH or metal series. Do not animate a measured concentration curve or turn the whole river red.

Keep protective structures and monitoring visible in M2–M5. The authority's response explains why sediment settling and lime controls alone do not establish prevention of longer-lived acid-generating processes. An icon for a pond is a control concept, not a calculated pollution-removal percentage. [LVV, printed pp.4–6](https://www.sttinfo.fi/files/69821631/72435808/532934/fi)

## 3. Operating water is a conditional future

At M6 show **air cooling: reported intent; final water budget not verified**. A simple loop and air-cooler icon are enough. Do not show active river abstraction or a maximum-demand counter by default. Air cooling does not undo the existing land/drainage impacts, and closed loops do not establish zero total site-water use. The technical alternatives come from the primary EIA; the reported intention comes from Kaleva. [EIA, printed pp.28–30](https://www.ymparisto.fi/sites/default/files/documents/Muhoksen%20palvelinkeskuksen%20arviointiohjelma.pdf), [Kaleva](https://www.kaleva.fi/googlen-datakeskukset-on-tarkoitus-jaahdyttaa-ilma/13668890)

An optional historical panel may show **TVE1: Oulujoki → cooling → evaporation and river return**, using the normalized 1,000 L example. It must say that this is an older planning alternative, not a live operating plant. The programme's local-waterworks capacity statement explains the proposed separate river source; it does not justify drying household taps or automatically building a larger municipal plant. [EIA, printed pp.28–30](https://www.ymparisto.fi/sites/default/files/documents/Muhoksen%20palvelinkeskuksen%20arviointiohjelma.pdf)

## Visual and interaction limits

- Keep only the active connection highlighted. The site and river provide the shared scene; avoid a full utility/catchment simulation.
- Use source-labelled solid connections for documented broad relationships and labelled schematic/dashed geometry for authored route shapes and future assets.
- At M2, show the actual pause context. At M3–M6, keep **Conditional future** visible; a user selecting Continue is not issuing a permit.
- Put numerical source provenance in details, while keeping critical labels directly beside figures: approximate area, gross rainfall, planning estimate and unknown operating demand.
- Replays do not accumulate water use, clearance, pollution or damage. Numeric unknowns stay unknown rather than zero.
