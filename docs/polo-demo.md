# Polo Fields guided demo

Open `/game.html?scenario=golden-gate-polo`. Heroes’ Grove remains at `/game.html?scenario=golden-gate-park`; links connect both experiences. `src/game/entry.ts` selects the independent Polo app, so the original sandbox’s geometry, 24 MW assumptions and controls are not reused for the research case.

The Polo demo reuses the full Golden Gate Park geographic pack, its local NAIP imagery/elevation, and optional streamed Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles. No new map download is needed when that pack is prepared. A local OSM place point positions a **schematic** 320 × 125 m teaching footprint. It is not a surveyed campus polygon. The model recipient, equipment, construction activity, underground connection and people are fictional illustrations. They do not establish real utility routes, a real pool connection or actual development plans.

## Prepared baseline

```sh
npm run data:prepare:polo
npm run data:verify:polo
```

Python 3 is needed for preparation/verification. The browser uses the committed `src/game/polo/baseline.generated.json`, so running the app requires no Python or live research downloads. The adapter reproduces Marian’s water and energy generators in temporary copies, validates their results and self-tests, and exports five English story chapters plus the seven original research snapshots in a separate `researchPhases` collection. It never rewrites the research packets. Tiny Python-version floating-point differences are checked within a numerical tolerance; text, shapes, unknowns and material values must match.

The baseline keeps the same 4 ha site, 1.2 ha roof and 10 MW **average** IT demand. Operation uses 12 MW annual-average facility demand, 105.12 GWh/year facility electricity and 300 m³/day average cooling make-up. These are authored research assumptions/calculations, not field measurements or forecasts. Work-stage figures cover selected activities and their stated time bases, not lifecycle totals. Unknown existing electricity, peak demand and grid headroom remain null. The new Foundations checkpoint has no separately assessed water/fuel inventory or runoff. In the retained research snapshots, stripping-only runoff is unknown and grading/compaction share the same runoff case. Rain animation explains the optional event and never changes annual electricity or creates a new weather forecast.

## Interaction and visual concepts

The player first positions a translucent campus directly on the map. The placement view shows the campus and a compact Confirm location button, without an introductory card or access-road overlay. Grab the campus itself or its green handle and drag; with it focused, use Left/Right arrows to rotate (Shift uses a finer step). The small rotate buttons provide an alternative. **Confirm location** locks the candidate, reveals the proposed access route and starts the animated Foundations chapter (M1). Today remains available as a comparison snapshot. Until confirmation, phases, research figures, access highlighting and traffic stay hidden and the server rejects requests to advance an unconfirmed plan.

After confirmation the player chooses when to advance through five chapters: **Today → Foundations → Structure → Ready → Operating**. Stable runtime IDs are M0, M1, M4, M5 and M6; M2/M3 are research-only snapshots, not selectable stops. The runtime M1 is a new foundation checkpoint and must not inherit stripping-only M1 figures. All stages, proposal comparisons and replays preserve the selected center and orientation. Confirmation locks both position and orientation for every phase, including the Today comparison. The Move data center handle, rotation controls and movement hints disappear, and pointer/keyboard placement is disabled. Restarting is the only way to choose a new location; it returns to the default unconfirmed candidate. Each state replaces the previous one; replay and skip never accumulate resource consumption. Orbit, zoom, whole-park framing, baseline rain and evidence remain available.

Moving forward plays a time-compressed illustration. Foundations takes 8 seconds: stripping, grading and compaction occupy the first 3 seconds, then foundation slabs appear over 5 seconds. Structure takes 9 seconds and starts from those completed foundations, raising steel before fitting roofs; its settled endpoint has an open frame and roof, without walls. Ready takes 7 seconds to add walls, paving, planting and commissioning. Operating takes 4 seconds to reveal flows on the completed campus. These are authored screen times, not a construction programme. Total playback is 28 seconds, down from 37 seconds; users control advancement. The forward action is labeled **Next phase**. Animation runs without a caption or progress strip inside the sidebar; a separate compact Skip control settles the scene immediately and Replay repeats the selected step. Backward navigation and non-adjacent timeline jumps show completed comparison snapshots; adjacent forward selection plays the next chapter. Reduced-motion preferences settle the work immediately. Changing rain does not restart construction. During transitions, inline cards describe the selected chapter’s model snapshot; shared work budgets and representative daily allowances are not simulated running totals.

### Inline chapter cards and resource story

All five chapters share the Today design: **General / Water / Electricity / Land & people** tabs inside the story panel. Selecting a topic opens no modal. The selected tab stays active when moving between chapters, with arrow keys, Home and End for keyboard navigation. General starts the walkthrough; Water and Electricity each show two or three explanatory sentences, one main quantity with its time basis, and a named comparison. Replay and Skip remain available. The separate optional Your impact dialog opens only on request; finishing construction or switching proposals no longer opens it automatically.

Foundations alone trials a revised layout: topic tabs at the top, then the chapter heading and the selected topic’s explanation in Maya’s speech bubble. This replaces its generic guide banner and the separate body paragraph. Other chapters retain the preceding layout while this version is refined.

The same Foundations trial replaces floating conversation invitations and individual avatar shortcuts with one larger **Talk** card. It opens a 2 × 2 chooser with one bold heading, portraits, larger names and roles; selecting a person opens the existing private chat. Its X closes the conversation and returns focus to Talk; there is no Back to people or bottom navigation panel. The chooser omits the eyebrow, description, repeated card calls to action and footer note. The Foundations private chat also omits its header eyebrow, subtitle and follow-up instruction, with a doubled portrait, larger title/message text and larger response cards; the composer and existing conversation behavior remain available. The illustrative crowd remains in the scene. **More info & data sources** is centered with larger text and a +/− disclosure indicator. The milestone bar and its labels are approximately 20% larger, with responsive spacing to keep it clear of the story card.

At the default 4 ha size, the water story shows **3.2M US gal/year** of modelled field irrigation; a shared **1.0M US gal selected site-water budget before opening** in Foundations and Structure; **57k US gal one-time commissioning water**, already inside that budget; and **30.2M US gal/year supplied to the operating campus**, about **9.5×** the irrigation reference. The shared budget sums the original non-overlapping research stages to 3,828 m³; showing it twice does not add consumption or say it has already been consumed. Offsite concrete mixing water (1,080 m³) is excluded. Operating supply includes 109,500 m³/year cooling, 1,095 domestic use and 3,600 irrigation. Cooling make-up is supplied water, not all evaporation: average-day supply / evaporation / managed blowdown are approximately 79k / 59k / 20k US gal for the selected evaporative cooling case. This is not a claim about every data centre.

Electricity uses representative model days: **0 kWh/day added by the new project** in Today, **240 kWh/workday** in Foundations and Structure, **2 MWh/test day** in Commissioning and **288 MWh/average operating day** for the whole facility. Existing park electricity remains unknown. The workday is 30 kW × 8 hours; applying it to Foundations is a new teaching assumption, without assigning a foundation duration or splitting the combined 160-day / 38.4 MWh construction inventory. A test day is 500 kW × 4 hours, **8.3×** the construction workday; operation is 12 MW × 24 hours, **1,200×** the construction workday (or 144× the test day). Operating power includes IT and all supporting systems; cooling is not added again. Ratios use unrounded quantities and name the comparison day rather than claiming a measured increase from park use.

Water cards put rain in a separate expandable explanation, with the existing replay control. The same 3.4-inch / 86.4 mm event sends **142k US gal** to drainage in Today and **585k US gal** for the finished campus, **4.1×** the original modelled drainage with the tank initially empty. Foundations runoff stays unquantified. Event capture is never subtracted from annual supplied water; water avoiding runoff is not all groundwater recharge or permanently retained water.

Quantities scale with selected area through the existing teaching model; ratios retain unrounded inputs. `resource-story.ts` derives the presentation quantities without changing generated baseline data or research packets. Assumptions, sources and original work snapshots remain in expandable inline details. Original-plan figures remain separate from visual proposal views, which show mechanisms and trade-offs without numeric savings. These are educational scenario values, not measurements or a validated Polo Fields forecast.

| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

## Optional construction interventions

Construction pauses to offer three optional, scripted improvements: protect a living-soil strip before clearing in Foundations; reinforce six marked sections halfway through Foundations; and paint planting plus a connected walking edge during Ready. Each offer has **Try improving it** and **Continue as planned**. There is no timer or penalty for declining. Drag over the map targets, tap them individually, or use Tab and Enter. The large keyboard legend and location-lock caption have been removed; map shortcuts still work outside interventions.

Painted sections change the 3D scene immediately. Completing every section commits one improvement, shows a short fictional community reaction and waits for **Resume construction**. Declining partway through removes that intervention's unfinished preview and resumes the same clock. Completed choices persist across chapters, comparisons and replays; Today hides later works. Replay can offer a previously declined moment again. Reset clears all choices. Keyboard navigation and camera dragging cannot accidentally move the map while painting. The existing protest introduction still happens before construction.

The protected strip remains visible and preparation machines avoid it. Reinforced sections are visible concrete additions in an authored check, not an assessment of real foundation strength. The planted walking edge grows trees, adds benches and a connected path, and brings walkers to the new route. These improvements do not restore the playing field or erase opposition. Map invitations, Community posts, authored private replies and live-chat context acknowledge the applied choices. Resource cards switch to qualitative effects and **Not recalculated**, rather than displaying unchanged original quantities as outcomes of the revised plan. No structural safety, water savings, carbon result or public consent is calculated.
